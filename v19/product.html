One board between your firmware and the world it thinks it lives in.

The DUT board carries your microcontroller. Augur One is everything your firmware expects to find around it: sensors that answer, loads that push back, buses that respond, and faults that arrive when the agent decides they should.

Three layers of computation, two of them on the board

Emulating a world has three jobs running at three speeds. Putting them on one processor is why bench rigs end up slow or unfaithful.

Layer 1 (on Augur One)

FPGA fabric: the part that has to be exact

Lattice ECP5 LFE5U-85F

Protocol timing is not a place for software. Register files, bus slaves, decode, fault injection and capture live in gateware, so the DUT's bus is answered at the DUT's own clock. This is what makes an MPU6000 look like an MPU6000 to a driver reading it at 8 kHz.

Its I/O banks run on four independently adjustable rails, 1.2 to 3.3 V, so a 1.8 V bus and a 3.3 V bus on the same DUT are both driven at their own level.

sensor models · protocol slaves · edge-accurate capture · fault injection
Layer 2 (on Augur One)

Real-time compute: the part that has to be fast

STM32N657, Cortex-M55

Physics is arithmetic, not timing. Plant models and closed-loop state update between the DUT's samples, close enough to the fabric to feed it. The NPU on this part is unused today; it is headroom for physics too expensive to integrate directly.

plant models · physics integration · closed-loop state
Layer 3 (off the board)

Bench computer: the part that has to think

Yours. Linux, gigabit Ethernet, nothing else

The agent, the tests, the verdicts and the evidence are not real-time work, so they sit off the board where they can be slow and restarted. The checker that scores runs lives here too, outside the loop it is judging.

the agent · test authoring · CI triggers · verdicts · evidence

One cable

Power over Ethernet is standard, so a bench is one run of Cat-6 and nothing else. USB-C 5 V is there when no PoE switch is.

Benches multiply: one per pull request, one per agent. A rack of one-cable benches is a thing a lab can actually operate.

It always comes back

A supervisor MCU on its own rail, with its own network path. Ethernet never drops during a recovery, so the FPGA and N6 stay powered and recovery is by reset.

N6 wedged
supervisor asserts its reset line.
FPGA misconfigured
PROGRAMN pulsed, reconfigured from NOR with I/O tristated.
Bitstream corrupt
the supervisor owns the FPGA's JTAG: SRAM-load a known-good image, or reprogram the config NOR through it.
Switch hangs
the one case where the supervisor resets the switch.

No power switches on the FPGA or N6, so there are no half-powered states to get stuck in. A bench you cannot recover remotely is a bench somebody has to walk to.
